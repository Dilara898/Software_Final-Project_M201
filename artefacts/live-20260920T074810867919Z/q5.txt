Question: How does CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing work at a molecular level?

CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing relies on a simplified version of a bacterial antiviral defense system, utilizing the Cas9 nuclease complexed with a synthetic guide RNA (gRNA) delivered into a cell [1]. Inside the cell's nucleus, the complex locks onto a short sequence known as the PAM, after which Cas9 unzips the DNA and matches it to the target RNA [7]. If the match is complete, the Cas9 enzyme uses molecular components to cleave or cut the DNA strands at the desired location [1, 5, 7]. Following this cleavage, the cell's natural repair mechanisms join the DNA ends back together [5]. This process allows existing genes to be removed, replaced, or new ones added in vivo [1, 5].
