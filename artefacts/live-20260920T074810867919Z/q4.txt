Question: What is the current state of fusion energy research?

Nuclear fusion research is actively ongoing across more than 50 IAEA member states, driven by impressive progress in fusion and plasma physics [8]. Major international collaborations include ITER, a megaproject utilizing a tokamak reactor to demonstrate magnetic confinement fusion and achieve sustained reactions where energy output exceeds input [7]. Additionally, programs like the U.S. Department of Energy's ALPHA program are exploring lower-cost, intermediate-density pulsed fusion approaches [4], while the DEMOnstration Power Plant (DEMO) is currently in the design stages to eventually supply fusion power [8]. Although putting fusion energy on the electrical grid remains a work in progress, the research has already advanced plasma physics and delivered real-world technological benefits [9].
