Question: What were the main causes of the 2008 financial crisis?

The 2008 financial crisis was triggered by a boom and bust cycle in the U.S. housing market and a banking crisis rooted in subprime mortgage lending [8, 9]. The main causes included excessive speculation on property values by both homeowners and financial institutions, which created the 2000s U.S. housing bubble [1]. This speculation was further fueled by loose credit, rampant market exuberance, and predatory lending practices for subprime mortgages [1, 9]. Additionally, these issues were exacerbated by deficiencies in regulatory oversight [1]. Austrian school economists also attributed the crisis to the Federal Reserve's prolonged policy of artificially low interest rates, which generated a credit bubble and widespread malinvestment [1].
