Question: What were the main causes of the 2008 financial crisis?

The 2008 financial crisis was primarily triggered by the collapse of the U.S. housing market and a banking crisis rooted in subprime mortgage lending [1, 2, 3]. This housing boom and subsequent bust were characterized by loose credit, predatory lending, and excessive speculation on property values by both homeowners and financial institutions [1, 3]. The situation was further exacerbated by deficiencies in regulation and the sudden drop in value of mortgage-backed securities tied to risky loans, which caused major financial institutions to fail and credit markets to freeze [1, 2]. Additionally, Austrian school economists attributed the underlying crisis to the Federal Reserve's prolonged policy of keeping interest rates artificially low [1].
