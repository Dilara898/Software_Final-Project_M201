Question: How does CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing work at a molecular level?

The CRISPR-Cas9 system relies on two primary components: a Cas9 endonuclease enzyme that acts as molecular scissors and a guide RNA sequence designed to match a target genetic location [1, 3]. Once inside a cell's nucleus, the complex locks onto a short sequence known as the PAM, where Cas9 unzips the DNA and matches it to its guide RNA [2]. If the guide RNA successfully pairs with the target sequence, Cas9 cleaves the target DNA strands [1, 2, 3]. Following the cut, the cell's natural DNA repair mechanisms are harnessed to join the severed ends, modifying the genome by adding, removing, or altering DNA sections [1, 3].
